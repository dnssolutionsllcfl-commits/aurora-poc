## SECTION 6, PROCESS (8-step framework)

**Section kicker:** How we work

**Section heading:**

> Eight steps from scope to live, plus the one most agencies skip.

**Section lead:**

> The shape of every engagement, in order. We do the same eight steps whether the work is a website, an automation, a monitoring deploy, or a fractional engagement. The eighth step is the one most firms skip, and it is the one our clients say matters most.

### Step 1, Discover

> A first call with the principals. No discovery deck, no sales call. We listen, we ask the two or three questions the answer to which decides whether we should take the work. End of call: yes, no, or come back when you have X.

### Step 2, Research

> Two to five days. We read your current site, your current tools, the team's actual operating constraints, and the market you sit inside. The output is a written research brief you can keep. We share it with you whether or not we move forward together.

### Step 3, Design

> Architecture, messaging hierarchy, visual direction. For a website, that means information architecture plus brand direction. For an automation, that means workflow map plus role definition. Design lands before build, and you sign off on it before a line of production code gets written.

### Step 4, Build

> The principals on the call, the AI team on the production work. Build runs in short cycles with a weekly receipt. You see the work in motion, not at the end. Production-ready code, not a prototype that has to be rebuilt before launch.

### Step 5, Automate

> Anything in the build that should run on its own gets handed to an agent with a scoped role and an audit log. This is where most engagements stop short. We don't. The automation step is the difference between a website and a working business system.

### Step 6, Test

> Lighthouse-grade performance, axe-core accessibility, end-to-end QA on every workflow. Every test is automated and runs on every change. If a deploy fails the gate, it doesn't ship. The standard is the same one we run on our own work.

### Step 7, Launch

> Public deploy, monitoring live, documentation handed over. We are on the line for the first week after launch by default. If something breaks, you talk to a principal, not a ticket queue. Launch day is not the end of the engagement.

### Step 8, Improve

> Most agencies disappear at launch. We don't. Every Aurora and Forge engagement has an improve cadence built in: monthly review, quarterly architecture audit, kill-criteria sign-off on anything we should stop doing. We run this on our own work first. This site is a weekly dogfood pass on the LP-meta build, and the same discipline runs on every client engagement after launch. The system stays useful as your business changes around it.
